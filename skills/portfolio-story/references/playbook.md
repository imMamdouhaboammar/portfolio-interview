## Reader decision and proof ladder

Write toward a concrete reader decision: interview this person, contact them, commission them, invite collaboration, or understand their expertise.

Use a proof ladder:
1. role/positioning,
2. relevant capability,
3. concrete work artifact,
4. verified outcome or evidence,
5. next action.

Do not substitute adjectives for missing proof. "Strategic, innovative, passionate" is not a case study.

## Headline system

A strong headline usually combines:
- who the person is or what they do,
- the problem/value domain,
- a differentiating proof or focus.

Generate multiple genuinely different angles, not synonym swaps. Examples of angle changes: role-led, outcome-led, domain-led.

Avoid unsupported superlatives and category claims such as "top", "leading", "best" unless the evidence contract supports them.

## Case-study architecture

For each project:

**Context:** what situation or constraint existed.

**Contribution:** what the person actually owned or did.

**Artifact/approach:** what was designed, built, researched, written, analyzed or delivered.

**Outcome:** only the verified result. If no measured outcome exists, end with the shipped artifact, adoption, decision or learning that is evidenced.

Team work should use language like "I led the research workstream" rather than "I transformed the company" unless that causal claim is supported.

## Claims and metrics

Attach source claim IDs to factual copy. Any number, date, employer, client, award, credential, quote or quantified outcome is source-sensitive.

When evidence is incomplete, prefer omission or a narrower accurate statement. Do not replace an unknown number with qualitative inflation.

## Arabic/English localization

Localization preserves function and facts, not syntax.

For Arabic:
- use the approved register/dialect,
- avoid calques from English,
- keep names/brands in their accepted form,
- preserve dates/numbers and units accurately,
- avoid inserting Latin punctuation patterns mechanically,
- make CTA wording natural to the audience.

For bilingual portfolios, the two versions may differ in rhythm and sentence length while remaining semantically equivalent.

## SEO/GEO copy integrity

Search metadata is still public factual copy. Do not invent:
- FAQ questions the person was never asked,
- testimonials or reviews,
- credentials,
- employer relationships,
- locations,
- service availability,
- awards.

Structured data should describe the actual page/person, not an aspirational persona.

## Revision strategy

On audit feedback, change the smallest coherent set:
- unsupported fact → remove or source it,
- vague headline → strengthen with existing proof,
- translation drift → repair both related locale fields,
- layout overflow → coordinate with design before deleting meaningful content.

Always return path-addressable changes so the build can be deterministic.
